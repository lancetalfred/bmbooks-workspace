Buying Back Second Hand Titles
Previous  Top  Next


The buyback option allows you to buy back books from customers in a point of sale environment with full cash and sales reporting.

ISBNs
Barcodes of second hand titles in Bookscan are recorded with the prefix 988 replacing the standard 978. So for ISBN 978040930309, second hand copies will be recorded as 988040930309.

Setting up the system for second hand titles
· Second hand titles all have supplier code BUYB. If it is not already on your database, add it in Supplier Maintenance.

· You may wish to set up a separate department , or departments, for second hand titles. This would allow financial tracking of second hand stock. If you do add a second hand department, we recommend that you give it the code BUY. (See the shortcut below).

· All titles for which you intend to stock second hand titles, can be entered manually in the title master. In the Barcode field, you will have to enter 988 and then the ISBN (less the check digit) as described in the paragraph above. Set the Cost Price field to be the average price you expect to pay to buy back the title. Set the Sell Price field to be the average price for which you expect to sell the title.

Important short cut: If you do not have the 988-prefixed barcode set up, the system will automatically create it for you when you first scan the 978-prefixed barcode into the Buyback window's barcode field. All the fields from the 978-prefixed barcodes master record will be copied to the second hand one, except that:
·"SECOND HAND COPY" will be appended to the end of the title,  
· you will be able to select the BUY department, 978-prefixed barcode's department or a different department  
·the Minimum Stock field will be set to zero.  
·the Firm Sale field will be set to true (i.e. no return to supplier allowed),  
To operate this feature the Auto create ISBN checkbox (just above the Barcode field) must be selected

Buying in second hand books
Step 1:
Open the Buy back window: from the main menu, click on Purchasing, then point to Incoming and then click on Second hand.

Step 2:
Scan the barcode on the book. The system will do one of two things:
· If the title has a 988-prefixed barcode, i.e. the title was previously sold as a second hand title, it will accept it into the system and display the title on the screen with the median cost price.  
· If the title has a 978-prefixed barcode, i.e. a normal barcode, Bookscan will translate it to a 988 prefix and look for the title in the title master. If it finds it, it displays the title and median cost price.  
·If it finds the 978-prefixed barcode but doesn't find the 988-prefixed barcode it, the system will offer to create the 988-prefixed barcode. You will need the Auto create ISBN checkbox selected for this to occur. (See the shortcut above).  

Step 3:
Determine from the condition of the book how much you wish to pay for it and overtype this amount n the Price field and click the Add button.
When all titles from this customer have been entered, click the Finished button. The total price will be displayed. Click the Finish button again to open the Tender Type window. Select the Tender Type and click OK.

Step 4:
Print the goods received note for the BUYB supplier and then post the stock and print the barcodes as when receiving standard stock. Cover the existing barcodes with the new 988 barcode labels. The labels will display the median price. As the sell price will vary with the condition of the book, you will need to strike through the price on the barcode label and write the appropriate sell price beside it..

Selling second hand titles
When second hand books are sold, the only difference is that you may need to overtype the sell price when it varies from the median.

Reconciling buy backs and sales
The end of day sales reports will detail buy back operations as follows:

All Sales Report
If you created buy back departments, sales of second hand titles will be reported under them.

Cashbook  
Titles purchased through the Buy back facility will display as tender type Second Hand Buy Back. They are "Cash Out" figures i.e. debt entries which are taken off the normal "Cash In" transactions so you can balance the cash drawer.
